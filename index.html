You know the feeling.

Nobody dropped the ball on purpose. There were just more balls than hands. The assistant exists for the gap between what you meant to do and what the day let you do.

  • The quote you meant to send Monday went out Thursday, after they'd bought elsewhere.
  • A supplier answered a week ago. The customer is still waiting on you.
  • "Can you get back to me by Friday?" sat on page three until Saturday.
  • The follow-up you promised yourself never happened, because the next fire did.

What it does, every day

These are the jobs it runs inside my company right now. Yours gets the ones that fit how you work.

Watches the inbox

Reads what comes in, sorts the noise from the money, and flags anything with a date in it. A request with a deadline becomes an alert on your phone the same hour.

Keeps follow-ups going

A steady number of touches every day, on the schedule you set. It stops the moment someone replies, so nobody gets a canned follow-up after they already wrote back.

Turns vendor quotes into customer quotes

When a supplier's price lands, it builds the customer quote in your format with your markup and puts it in your drafts, ready to send the same day.

Protects your relationships

It knows who your customers are and never treats one like a stranger. Existing accounts stay out of every automated list unless you release them.

Does the recurring paperwork

Expense reports, weekly activity summaries, the Monday numbers for the team. Drafted on time, from the real records, without being asked twice.

Remembers everything

Every promise, markup, visit and "never contact that supplier again" lives in its memory on your server. Ask it anything about an account and get the answer, not a search.

Works where you already work

Your email, calendar, CRM, files and chat. You talk to it in a chat window on your phone. There is nothing new to learn and no portal to log in to.

Why this is not another app

Software companies sell you a tool and leave. This is an assistant with an operator behind it. I build it, I watch it, and when it gets something wrong I fix the rule so it never happens again.

It runs on your own server

A small private server you own, on your own Claude subscription, behind your own private network. Your email and records never pass through my systems. If we ever part ways, you keep everything.

Approval is a setting, not a limit

Every new job starts with you approving each action. When you trust it, you turn approval off for that job and it runs on its own. You decide how fast that happens.

You never touch the technical side

No terminal, no configuration files, no passwords sent to me. I will never ask you for one. You get a chat window and a phone that buzzes when something matters.

It only uses the front door

It connects to your services the way those companies allow. No tricks with your accounts, nothing that puts them at risk.

How it starts

Two weeks from the first call to an assistant that knows your business.

  1. A 30-minute call

    You tell me how the business actually runs, what eats your day, and where you'd want it to check with you first. We decide which jobs come first.

  2. Your assistant interviews you

    I stand it up and it asks you the questions itself, in plain English. It writes its own understanding of your company, your people and your rules from that conversation. I review and shape it.

  3. Week one, setup

    Everything connected, the first jobs running, approval on for all of them. You start talking to it from your phone.

  4. Week two, dial-in

    Version one is always a little wrong. It learns who matters, what to flag and what to leave alone, and we tighten the rules together.

  5. Then it just runs

    Care covers the fixes, the new rule when something slips, and the changes as your business changes. One business day to hear back from me, usually much less.

Questions owners ask

Which AI is it?

Claude, made by Anthropic, running on a server you own and a subscription in your name. Your conversations and records are not used to train anyone's model when the setting is turned off, which I do on day one.

Can it see my email?

Yes, that's the job. It reads your mail on your server, in your account. I can only see what you choose to show me, and I will never ask you for a password.

Will it send things without asking?

Not until you say so. Every job starts in approval mode. Most owners switch the routine jobs to run on their own within a few weeks and keep approval on for anything that goes to a customer.

What does it connect to?

Whatever you run the business on. Microsoft 365 or Google, your calendar, your CRM, your files, your chat app, your accounting. We connect what you have, and there is no limit on how many.

What if I don't like it?

Care is month to month. You own the server and the accounts, so if you stop, nothing disappears and nothing is held hostage.

What happens when it gets something wrong?

It will, especially in the first weeks. You tell me, I find out why, and I write the rule that stops it from happening again. That loop is the product.

Where are you?

Middle Tennessee. Everything on this page is done remotely, so where you are doesn't matter.
